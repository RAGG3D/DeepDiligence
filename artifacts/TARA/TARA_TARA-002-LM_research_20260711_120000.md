# TARA-002-LM — Protara Therapeutics (TARA)

Research cutoff: 2026-07-11. Sources are Protara regulatory and clinical disclosures, STARBORN-1 NCT05871970, the ISSVA 2026 poster, FDA materials, and peer-reviewed sclerotherapy publications.

### Chapter 1: Drug Overview

TARA-002 is an intracystic inactivated *Streptococcus pyogenes* immunopotentiator produced from the same master cell bank as OK-432. In lymphatic malformations it is intended to generate a local inflammatory response that collapses the cystic lesion. STARBORN-1 is a recruiting Phase 2a/b study in patients aged six months to under 18 years with macrocystic or mixed cystic head, neck or mediastinal lymphatic malformations. FDA has granted Rare Pediatric Disease, Orphan Drug, Fast Track and Breakthrough Therapy designations, and Protara stated that it intends to submit a BLA in the second half of 2027.

**Target:** Local TLR2/NOD2-linked immunopotentiation in cystic lymphatic malformations

Economic share: 100%

Sources: Protara Q1 2026 update dated 2026-05-13; STARBORN-1 NCT05871970; Protara 2025 Form 10-K.

### Chapter 2: Forecasting Strategy

Only macrocystic and mixed cystic pediatric lymphatic malformations are forecast. Microcystic-predominant disease is excluded by the clinical protocol and is not embedded in the TAM. TARA-002-LM is modeled separately from TARA-002-NMIBC because its regulatory path is materially earlier.

#### 3.1.3 Competitive Landscape

No drug is FDA-approved specifically for lymphatic malformations. Current care includes image-guided sclerotherapy with doxycycline, bleomycin, ethanol or sodium tetradecyl sulfate, surgery, and off-label systemic agents for selected molecular disease. A randomized U.S. OK-432 study reported complete or substantial response in 68% of 117 intent-to-treat patients, including 94% in macrocystic and 63% in mixed disease. A 48-study review found 89% overall sclerotherapy success, with 89% in macrocystic and 71% in mixed lesions. Vijoice is FDA-approved for severe PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum rather than all LM and produced a 27% radiologic response at week 24 in 37 patients.

Sources: Smith et al., Laryngoscope 2009, PMID 19117316; systematic review PMID 35151497; FDA Vijoice approval dated 2022-04-05.

#### 3.1.5 Differentiation Assessment

Assessment: Tier One — potentially the first standardized FDA-approved localized LM therapy, but the small single-arm dataset and effective generic procedures preclude a BIC forecast today.

At the April 10, 2026 cutoff, STARBORN-1 had treated 16 patients: 12 completed treatment, 10 were evaluable at eight weeks, all 10 met clinical success and 80% achieved success with one or two doses. Seven patients had reached 32 weeks and remained disease-free. TEAEs were generally mild to moderate, but the ISSVA table reported 1/16 (6%) Grade 3 TEAE and related Grade 3 TEAE; there were no serious TEAEs or adverse events of special interest. The evidence is promising but too small and uncontrolled to displace established, inexpensive sclerotherapy broadly at launch.
