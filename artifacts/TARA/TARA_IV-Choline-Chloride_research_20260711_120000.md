# IV Choline Chloride — Protara Therapeutics (TARA)

Research cutoff: 2026-07-11. Sources are Protara's 2025 Form 10-K and May 2026 corporate presentation, THRIVE-3 NCT06910943, company releases, and peer-reviewed human choline-replacement studies.

### Chapter 1: Drug Overview

IV Choline Chloride is an investigational phospholipid substrate-replacement therapy for adolescent and adult patients dependent on long-term parenteral support when oral or enteral nutrition is not possible, insufficient or contraindicated. Protara currently describes THRIVE-3 as a registrational Phase 3 trial with a seamless Phase 2b/3 design: a 24-patient open-label dose-confirmation portion followed by approximately 105 randomized patients; its primary endpoint is change in plasma choline concentration. The first patient was dosed in January 2026 and an interim analysis is expected in the second half of 2026. Protara states that no IV choline product is approved globally and that U.S. compound and method-of-treatment patents extend to 2041.

**Target:** Replacement of deficient circulating choline during long-term parenteral support

Economic share: 100%

Sources: Protara release dated 2026-01-07; THRIVE-3 NCT06910943; Protara May 2026 corporate presentation.

#### 3.1.3 Competitive Landscape

No IV choline product is approved globally. Current parenteral-support formulations supply insufficient free choline, and oral/enteral replacement is not viable for the target label. Clinical competition is therefore indirect: nutritional management, lipid formulations and treatment of downstream liver disease. A placebo-controlled 2001 study randomized 15 long-term TPN patients to usual TPN or 2 g/day choline chloride for 24 weeks and reported separation in liver CT density by week four, supporting biologic activity but not commercial adoption.

Sources: Buchman et al., JPEN 2001, PMID 11531217; Protara May 2026 corporate presentation; Protara 2025 Form 10-K.

#### 3.1.5 Differentiation Assessment

Assessment: Best-in-Class — the only targeted IV product in a no-approved-therapy market, with first-mover and workflow advantages despite commercial and surrogate-endpoint risk.

IV Choline Chloride would be the first standardized intravenous replacement for an already guideline-recognized deficiency. The registrational path is relatively compact, intellectual property extends to 2041, and treatment can be incorporated into existing parenteral-support workflows. The principal risks are reliance on a plasma biomarker primary endpoint, uncertain payer willingness to support rare-disease pricing, and the lack of current controlled outcomes data on liver or functional benefit.
